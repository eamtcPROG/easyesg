The settings tier, where consequences are named

Six screens the owner-administrator visits a handful of times a year, each of which can change what a colleague sees inside a report. Entities, periods, identifiers, access and the plan. Nothing here is a form for its own sake: every save, lock, removal and downgrade states the object it touches and what it does to the work already in progress.

S-14 · UC-56 … UC-58 · UX-71, UX-72

Reporting periods — locking, and the amendment that shows

Locking is the only action in the tenant application that takes editing away from colleagues, so it is worded as what it does to them, not as a status change. Reopening is possible, requires a reason, and leaves a mark that stays visible on the period and in the export — an amendment must look like an amendment.

Why the counter is not a gate

The block sits at creation, the one boundary where waiting costs nothing. No limit is ever enforced inside a report: a draft started on any plan stays editable, exportable and downloadable, including after a downgrade or a lapse. In May, with a deadline four days away, this is the difference between a nuisance and a disaster.
