Money leaves the product, then comes back with an answer

Nine screens between choosing a plan and holding an invoice. The hard part is not the order form; it is the round trip through a bank the platform does not control, and the four ways it can end. No card field exists anywhere here. Every amount is shown net, VAT and gross before anyone confirms, every unavailable rail says why it is unavailable, and every state the user can return to has a screen of its own.

Why an interstitial at all

A silent redirect to a differently-branded page is the moment users abandon payment, because it looks like an interception. Naming the bank before the jump makes the bank's page a confirmation rather than a surprise.

The order number appears here and nowhere earlier, deliberately: it is the string support will ask for, and it must be readable from the last screen the user saw before things went wrong.
Never in this product
The card form that does not exist
no card fields · PCI SAQ-A

No screen in EasyESG accepts a card number, expiry, CVV or cardholder name — not in checkout, not in a settings screen, not in support tooling. Whether a card is stored at all is the provider’s business with the customer.

The reference as the primary object

In Moldovan practice the transfer is initiated in the bank's own app, often by a different person than the one who placed the order. The reference is therefore the payload of the whole screen: 38 px monospace, one-click copy, and an amount and IBAN that can be read aloud over a phone.

The proforma exists because accounting departments will not release a payment without a document, and it says on its face that it is not a fiscal invoice — the invoice comes after the money, which is what the tax treatment requires.
Out of scope · owned by the payment provider
Cards and recurring consent live at the bank

EasyESG has no screen for saving, listing or removing a card, and no consent checkbox of its own. The card is entered on the provider's page, the recurring mandate is taken there, and it is withdrawn there — so there is no state for this product to hold, display or get wrong.

What EasyESG shows instead is the consequence, where the consequence is felt: the renewal date and amount on Subscription history, and a reminder 14 days before each charge. If a renewal is not collected, the order reopens and the notification centre says so.

Suspension is not deletion, and must not look like it

The red banner is the strongest visual state in the product and it is used exactly once, here, because the user's compliance deadline may be days away. Two dates are shown, not one: when editing stopped, and when the retention clock turns the data read-only. Users who read the first as the second panic; users who see both plan.
